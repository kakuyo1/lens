The quiet architecture of serendipity

Mara Ellison · October 2026

A study of three European cities suggests that much of what people call a happy accident is really a product of street layout. Blocks that funnel foot traffic past shops, benches and small squares produced far more unplanned meetings than wide, car-oriented avenues.

The authors tracked more than two thousand volunteers over a year, using phones that logged where they walked and who they passed. Their conclusion is modest: planners cannot schedule luck, but they can make it likelier.
