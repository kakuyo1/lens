# 0203 The glass idle release trims the shared device

- **Status**: accepted (2026-11-20; the memory-optimization campaign, the experiment after ADR 0202's floor)
- **Background**: ADR 0202 released the capture sessions 10 s after the last glass sheet hid. That returned the AMD capture threads and about 1,200 handles, but left a private floor of +42.0 to +144.8 MB above idle across its six post-release runs: Heap stayed flat at 158.9–161.7 MB, threads and handles returned to their post-show level, and `IDXGIDevice3::Trim()` on the capture device changed nothing. The residual therefore sat in the drivers' per-process high-water, and the one device still holding glass-time allocations was the shared device every panel renders on (docs/adr/0191), not the capture device on the AMD adapter.
- **Decision**:
  - `SharedGraphics::trim()` calls `IDXGIDevice3::Trim()` on the shared device, and does nothing when no device exists (or off Windows, where the class is a forward declaration only).
  - `GlassBackdrop::setIdleTrim()` takes the step; `main.cpp` passes `sharedGraphics.trim()` through it, so the glass runs the composition root's device rather than looking up a process-wide one. `SharedGraphics` is declared before the controller for that wiring to outlive it.
  - The step runs in `releaseIdleSessions()`, after one release per screen session is queued: the refresh has been stopped for 10 s and no sheet is live. The pet and the pencil layer keep their own devices (ADR 0191), so the call touches no continuously rendering surface.
- **Consequences**:
  - On the release tree, same machine, same reader settings, same protocol (idle 60 s, show settings, hold 10 s, hide, sample to hide+120 s): seven rounds without the trim left +31.2 to +56.2 MB at hide+60 s (median +41.6); four with it left +12.5 to +16.9 MB (median +13.7). The two distributions do not overlap (Mann-Whitney exact p = 0.006); the median drop is 27.9 MB, and every trimmed round lands inside the campaign's +50 MB line with 33 MB to spare. At hide+120 s the medians are +41.5 and +12.9 MB. The trim line is written 8–9 ms after the release line in every round.
  - It lowers memory only: the ~28 driver threads and ~120 handles that stay after the release are the same in both arms. What remains above idle after the trim is the ignition's own residual, and the wide spread of the un-trimmed floors is not explained by frame count (three rounds of 668–675 frames left +42.6, +55.5 and +144.8 MB the same evening, and seven rounds here with 624–1,935 frames spanned +31.2 to +56.2). The spread most likely belongs to whether the captured desktop kept changing during the window, which this decision does not measure.
  - The hint is not a free: the driver keeps what it cannot rebuild cheaply, and the next card may pay to rebuild the rest. Measured reopening did not move: 116–127 ms to paint and 140–167 ms to a new capture line with the trim, inside the 99–147 ms and 150–176 ms the untrimmed rounds showed. The call itself returns 8–9 ms after the release line, on the GUI thread, at a moment when no sheet is live.
- **Alternatives rejected**:
  - **Trim only the capture device** (ADR 0202's B, C and E rounds): no effect on the floor; the device that holds the allocations is the shared one.
  - **Share the capture device with the shared device**: still the larger change across ADR 0191 and ADR 0087, and the trim alone recovers the measured part of the floor at one call.
  - **Throttling the refresh so fewer frames are copied back**: the frame count does not explain the floor, so it would cost the live picture for nothing.
